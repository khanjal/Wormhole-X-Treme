# Wormhole Research Facility — restyling inspiration digest

Research date: 2026-10-01. Read-only web research; nothing was downloaded. Ideas are described in my own words; each is tied to the page that prompted it. Everything proposed uses blocks that exist in Minecraft 1.20 (no copper bulbs, tuff bricks, crafters, trial-chamber blocks, or anything else from 1.21+).

Working premise: the facility already has the right bones (white concrete/quartz shell, copper for the operable bits, glass, sea lanterns, one department colour per wing). What it lacks is *theatre* — depth in the walls, a sense of weight and procedure around the gate, a reason to look up, and set-piece props that say "someone works here". The references below are chosen for that.

## 2. Ideas worth borrowing, by area

### 2a. Gate Room (Ops atrium)

1. **Give the gate a ramp and a pit.** (Refs 5, 7.) The SGC gate sits on a plinth with a long shallow ramp up the centreline; everything else in the room is lower. Build: raise the gate base 2 blocks on a 7-wide platform of polished deepslate or smooth stone; run a 3-wide ramp of stone-brick or deepslate-tile *stairs* (full stairs, not slabs, so the treads read as grating) from the floor to the platform, flanked by iron-bar railings on a 1-block kerb of polished blackstone. Grey, not white: the ramp is the one dark object in a white room and the eye goes straight to it.

2. **Opposing blast doors, not an open arch.** (Refs 5, 7.) The SGC room has blast doors on two opposite walls. Build each as a 3-wide, 4-high opening framed in a 1-block surround of polished blackstone with a yellow/black hazard band (yellow concrete and black concrete alternating, 1 block each, as the bottom row of the frame and as a 1-block stripe on the floor across the threshold). Fill the opening with iron-bar-topped iron doors or, for the "shut" state, a 3x4 panel of iron blocks on a piston column if redstone is welcome. Even if they never close, the frame says they could.

3. **Control-room window opposite the gate, one level up.** (Refs 5, 7, 8.) Face the gate with a long window (at least 7 wide, 2 high) of plain glass at second-floor height, with a 1-block sill of cut copper and, inside, a row of consoles facing the glass. The SGC can lower a blast shield over it; echo that with a strip of iron trapdoors hinged open above the window, as if the shutter were parked.

4. **Briefing room above the control room, with its own window.** (Refs 5, 7.) The SGC's briefing room overlooks the gate room through a second, larger window one level higher. If the atrium is tall enough, add a third floor: a long table (dark-oak slabs on dark-oak fence legs, or a 1x7 line of smooth-quartz slabs), chairs (stairs), a map wall of item-frame maps, and glass down to the sill. Walking up past two windows before reaching the floor of the gate room, as a visitor would, is the single most SGC-feeling sequence available.

5. **A ceiling that pretends it opens.** (Refs 5, 7.) The SGC gate room is the bottom of a silo. Directly over the gate, inset a square of the ceiling by one block and fill it with a grid of iron trapdoors or a 2-tone square of light-grey and grey concrete with chains hanging to a crane beam of polished blackstone. Nothing needs to move; it just explains why the room is tall.

6. **Light the gate, not the room.** (Refs 13, lighting search.) Replace surface sea lanterns around the gate with hidden ones: a double-skin wall with sea lanterns or end rods behind iron bars / glass panes, and `light` blocks (creative-only `/give`) for anything that must stay invisible. Keep one deliberate visible light source: a pair of soul lanterns or blue-stained-glass-backed sea lanterns either side of the gate to put a cold tint on the ring itself.

7. **An iris story without an iris.** (Ref 6.) Around the inside of the gate's inner track, run a ring of iron trapdoors (open, flush to the ring) or a ring of cut-copper slabs to suggest the housing that the plates retract into; put a single lectern with a written "IDC log" book at the control-room console as the palm reader.

### 2b. Labs and test chambers

8. **Gate Dynamics test stand: an observation gallery that looks like an observation gallery.** (Refs 5 isolation-ward lounge, 7.) The SGC isolation ward is watched from a lounge directly opposite through a window. Set the observation deck one block higher than the stand, behind glass, with a continuous cut-copper sill and a 1-block-deep recess under the window of polished-deepslate; put a line of iron trapdoors (closed, flush) along the top of the window as the "blast shutter rail". The deck gets its own hazard stripe on the floor at the door.

9. **Six-gate gallery as a museum, with plinths and labels.** (Ref 4 for cheap gates, Ref 8 for text on steps.) Give each gate shape its own 1-block plinth of polished basalt (dark, vertically grained, reads as machined) with a hanging sign (1.20) on an iron-chain bracket naming the shape, and a different light colour per shape — a stained-glass pane in front of a sea lantern under each plinth edge. Atlantis writes text on its stair risers; write the shape's name on the riser of the step up to each plinth using a row of signs.

10. **Ring Transit shaft: make the drop visible and dangerous.** (Ref 2 ring platforms; hazard search.) Line the 60-deep shaft with alternating 4-block bands of light-grey concrete and polished deepslate so the fall is countable through the glass window; put a hazard-striped 1-block collar (yellow/black concrete) at every opening, and a glowing floor ring at the bottom (sea lantern under a ring of tinted glass, so it reads as a dim landing pad, not a bright floor). The glass window gets a deepslate-tile frame and a copper sill like the gate-room window, so every "look into a dangerous volume" moment in the facility uses the same language.

11. **Beam Physics pad array: borrow the Aperture test-chamber grid.** (Portal search; Ref 11 trio palette.) Beam pads sit in a chamber whose walls are a 2x2 panel grid: smooth quartz panels separated by 1-block grooves of light-grey concrete with an end rod recessed into every fourth groove. Each pad is a 3x3 of cut-copper slabs on a ring of polished-blackstone with one glow-lichen or sea-lantern-under-stained-glass centre, numbered with a sign on a copper-block bollard. The dispatch office overlooks the array through the standard window.

12. **Mirror Optics gallery: tinted glass and amethyst.** (Ref 12 blue-glass telescope.) Niches become 3-wide, 4-high recesses framed in polished deepslate with a tinted-glass back wall in front of a hidden sea lantern (so each niche glows faintly without casting light into the hall) and an amethyst-cluster or amethyst-block "sample" on a lectern in front. Floor of the hall in polished-diorite and calcite checker so the mirrors have something to reflect visually.

13. **Containment-look cells where things could go wrong.** (brainbound summary; Ref 11 sculk lab.) Beam and Mirror labs each get one "failed experiment" alcove behind cyan stained glass: sculk, sculk veins and a sculk sensor on deepslate, lit red (redstone-lamp behind red stained glass). It gives the test campus a story and a reason for the warning signage.

### 2c. Corridors and wayfinding

14. **One corridor section, repeated.** (Sci-fi corridor search: modularity; Ref 13 depth.) Define a 3-wide, 4-high corridor module and build every corridor from it: walls of white concrete with a 1-block recessed band at eye height (light-grey concrete), a floor of light-grey concrete with a 1-wide centre line in the wing's colour (concrete), ceiling of smooth-quartz slabs with an end rod every 4 blocks in a 1-block channel, and a vertical "rib" every 6 blocks — a column of polished-deepslate 1 block proud of the wall with an iron-trapdoor vent on it. Flat corridors are what make the current build feel like a hospital.

15. **Floor lines do the wayfinding.** (Wayfinding search; SCP search for black/white zoning.) The department colour already exists; move it to the floor. A 1-wide coloured concrete line runs from the Gate Room atrium down the centre of each corridor to its wing, splitting at junctions. Where two wings share a corridor, run two lines side by side. Signage then only needs to confirm, not explain.

16. **Zone the facility by wall tone.** (SCP search: heavy zone / light zone.) Keep white concrete for Ops and the "clean" labs; switch to light-grey concrete with deepslate ribs for Ring Transit and the Menagerie/Motor Pool; use polished-blackstone for the Nether Range's internal walls. Three tones, so you always know roughly where you are without reading a sign.

17. **Hazard stripes at every threshold that matters.** (Hazard search.) One row of alternating yellow and black concrete across the floor of every lab door, blast door, shaft collar and pad edge, and nowhere else. Diagonal stripes are not possible in blocks; the 1:1 alternation reads correctly at Minecraft scale.

18. **Signage that is read, not just placed.** (1.20 hanging signs; Ref 8 stair text.) Hanging signs under copper-chain brackets at each junction, room-number signs on a dark-grey (grey concrete) plaque beside every door, and an item-frame map wall in the Gate Room showing the campus floor plan.

### 2d. Lighting

19. **Hide the sources.** (Lighting search; Ref 13.) Two-skin walls with sea lanterns or end rods behind iron bars, glass panes or slabs; `light` blocks where nothing can show. Visible lights become deliberate props.

20. **Colour-code by temperature.** Cool white (sea lantern, end rod) for Ops and the clean labs; warm (lantern, copper-shaded glowstone behind orange glass) for the Menagerie/Motor Pool and the Systems mezzanine; blue (soul lantern, soul fire in a soul-sand pit, or sea lantern behind blue stained glass) for anything about the wormhole itself; red (redstone lamp behind red stained glass) only for alarms and containment.

21. **Froglights as instrument lamps.** Ochre/verdant/pearlescent froglights are small, bright, flat-faced and 1.20-legal; use them as single indicator lamps on consoles and above doors instead of full sea lanterns.

22. **Under-lit edges.** Put the light under the lip of a platform, sill or plinth (sea lantern inset one block below a slab overhang) rather than in the ceiling; the Beam pads, gate plinth and gallery plinths all get this.

### 2e. Palettes and block combinations that read as "sci-fi research base" in 1.20

23. **Clean shell:** white concrete (walls, not quartz: no grid lines), smooth quartz (ceilings and slabs), light-grey concrete (recess bands, floors), polished diorite / calcite (floor checker in galleries). (brainbound summary; Ref 11.)

24. **Machined dark:** polished deepslate and deepslate tiles (floors, ramp, frames), polished basalt (plinths, vertical-grained columns), polished blackstone (kerbs, blast-door surrounds, Nether interiors). These are the "steel" of 1.20.

25. **Operable copper, oxidised with intent:** keep plain copper for what you touch, but use *exposed* and *weathered* cut copper for the trim around it and *oxidised* copper for anything older or Atlantis-coded. Wax what should stay a given colour. (Refs 9, 10.) Lightning rods are 1.20 copper "antennae" for roofs and console backs.

26. **Glass with a job:** plain glass for windows you look through, tinted glass where you want a glow without light spill, cyan stained glass for containment, blue stained glass over sea lanterns for "wormhole" light, white stained glass panes as diffusers in front of end rods.

27. **Instruments:** observers (the face reads as a sensor), daylight detectors (flat panel with a grid — ideal console tops), target blocks (a dial), note blocks, lecterns with books, item frames holding maps/compasses/clocks/spyglasses, chiseled bookshelves (1.20) as rack-mounted equipment, iron trapdoors as vents and panels, chains and iron bars as cable trays, glow item frames as back-lit readouts, armour stands with helmets as suited personnel.

28. **Atlantis accent set (for the End Annex or one mirror hall):** oxidised/weathered copper, orange-and-cyan stained glass in a geometric panel, warped-stem or dark-prismarine for the blue-green end of the copper spectrum, red terracotta floor (Price Tower's Cherokee red), purpur for the Ancients' pale lavender stone. (Refs 9, 10.)

29. **Nether Range set:** polished blackstone, basalt (natural, for columns), gilded blackstone as the only highlight, soul fire / soul lanterns for all lighting, crimson stem for the one warm wood, chains everywhere, ancient-debris-textured accents not available but netherite blocks are if you want one unmistakable "this cost something" block at the gate plinth. (Ref 16.)

### 2f. Set-piece details

30. **Console module** (Refs 8, 14, 15.) A 1-high L of smooth-quartz stairs facing the operator (the sloped front), a daylight-detector or observer on top as the screen, an item frame with a map on the wall behind, a froglight indicator, and a lever/button on cut copper. Three of these in a row behind the gate-room window is a control room; one is a workstation.

31. **Monitor wall.** A 3x2 or 5x3 panel of black concrete framed in light-grey concrete, with item-frame maps as the picture and glow item frames for "live" screens. A single map of the facility, placed in every control room, doubles as wayfinding.

32. **Blast door.** 3x4 opening, polished-blackstone surround, yellow/black band at floor, iron doors or a piston-driven iron-block panel, a redstone-lamp "door status" above the frame (red glass over a lamp), a button on a copper block beside it. (Refs 1, 2, hazard search.)

33. **Warning signage.** A 2x1 plaque of yellow concrete framed in black concrete with a sign on it; at the Beam array, shaft collars, test stand and Nether gate. Keep the text short — a single word plus a number — since signs are small.

34. **Equipment racks.** Chiseled bookshelves stacked 2 high in a deepslate frame with a chain down the side and an end rod along the top; fill selected slots so the "lit" slots differ from rack to rack.

35. **The self-destruct / interlock gag.** (Ref 5.) A 2-key station: two levers on cut-copper blocks 5 blocks apart, both feeding a redstone AND into a single redstone lamp. Nothing else; it is the kind of prop people photograph.

36. **MALP garage → Motor Pool.** (Ref 5.) Service bays with an iron-trapdoor roller-door motif (a row of iron trapdoors above each bay opening), a floor drain (hopper or a 1-block pit with iron bars), hazard stripes at bay edges, and minecarts/boats parked on rails or in a dry dock of stairs. The rail yard gets a copper-and-chain gantry over the tracks.

37. **Menagerie / stables that are still a lab.** Pens in iron bars on polished-deepslate kerbs rather than wood fences, each pen with a hanging sign and a lectern "log". Kennel with a soul-campfire (safe) warmth prop and a water trough (cauldron). Boathouse: a wet dock with a tinted-glass floor over lit water.

### 2g. Exterior and far sites

38. **Nether Range: a repaired bastion / fortress outpost.** (Ref 16.) Build the Range into or against a fortress or bastion silhouette: nether brick and blackstone exterior, soul-fire lighting, a long approach bridge with chain railings and hazard stripes, the gate plinth in polished blackstone with gilded-blackstone corners. Internal walls in polished blackstone keep the corridor module but in the dark tone (idea 16).

39. **End Annex: an orbital station around the gateway beams.** (Refs 12, 16.) Build the Annex as a ring or spoke station floating off the main island, oriented so one or more end-gateway beams pass through or beside its windows. Palette: purpur, end-stone brick, obsidian spines (1-block-thick vertical obsidian pillars are the End City signature), copper/oxidised copper trim in the Atlantis set (idea 28), a transparent (glass or tinted-glass) bridge from the arrival point. Chorus plants inside a glass "garden" column as the counterpoint to the tech (Ref 11 garden dome).

40. **Atlantis stained glass behind the far-site gates.** (Refs 8, 9.) Behind the End gate, a tall geometric window of orange, cyan, light-blue and white stained glass in a 30-60-style zigzag (approximated with stair-stepped diagonals), back-lit by hidden sea lanterns. It is the one place in the facility to go flamboyant, and it marks the Annex as the "other" architecture.

41. **Exterior of the main campus.** Follow the Price Tower idea at campus scale: a vertical "trunk" (the Ops atrium) with cantilevered wings, copper louvers (a row of waxed cut-copper slabs or stairs set 1 block proud over each window band), tinted-glass bands, and white concrete between. Lightning rods on the roof read as antennae.

## 3. What the real Stargate sets do that the facility could echo

The SGC (Refs 5, 6, 7) is a bunker: a square concrete room at the bottom of a missile silo, with the gate on a plinth and a long ramp running up to it so vehicles can drive through. Heavy blast doors sit on opposite walls, and above the gate the silo ceiling can open to lift the gate out. The gate is dialled not from a DHD but from a control room one floor up and directly opposite, looking down through a wide window; that window has its own blast shield that drops when something hostile is coming. One floor above that again is the briefing room, a long table in front of a second, larger window, so the commanding officer watches the gate from his meeting. The iris is a set of more than twenty metal leaves that slide out from the inner ring, and opening it requires a palm scan at the control-room console. Elsewhere on the levels are a MALP garage with service bays, an isolation ward watched from a lounge opposite through glass, a power vault behind a coded fence, and a self-destruct that needs two officers with keys at opposite ends of the device. The whole thing is grey concrete, steel grating, caged lights and painted level numbers — dark and claustrophobic by design.

Atlantis (Refs 8, 9, 10) was deliberately the opposite: open and airy. The gate stands at the foot of a wide split-level room dominated by a grand staircase; the control room is up on a balcony that overlooks the gate at an angle rather than head-on, with the conference room and the commander's office on the same upper level and the jumper bay above that. Behind the gate is a huge stained-glass window. The production designer modelled the city on Frank Lloyd Wright — specifically the Price Tower — so everything is built on a 30-60-90 triangle grid with angular geometry, and circles are reserved for machines like the gate. The palette is copper in every oxidation state, from bright red-brown through to blue-green, against warm red floors and tinted glass. Even the stair risers carry text: an Ancient welcome inscription runs up the steps to the control room.

What the facility can take: the *vertical sequence* of the SGC (ramp up to the gate; window above; second window above that), the *ceremony* of thresholds (blast doors, hazard bands, shutters), the *dark-against-white* contrast of grating and steel in a clean room, and — for one wing or the End Annex — Atlantis's copper-and-stained-glass warmth as a counterpoint, with text on the steps.

## 4. Top 10 changes, ordered by impact on how it feels to walk through

1. **[Ops / Gate Room]** Put the gate on a plinth with a dark stair-ramp up the centreline and iron-bar railings; everything else in the room stays low.
2. **[Ops / Gate Room]** Build the control room as a window wall one floor up, opposite the gate, with three console modules behind it and a parked iron-trapdoor shutter above.
3. **[All corridors]** Replace flat corridors with one repeated module: recessed eye-height band, deepslate rib with vent every 6 blocks, end-rod channel in the ceiling, department colour as a floor line.
4. **[All wings]** Move lighting into the walls and under lips; visible lights become deliberate props in one of four colours (white, warm, blue, red).
5. **[Ops / Gate Room]** Opposing blast doors with polished-blackstone surrounds, hazard bands and door-status lamps; the same door detail at every lab entrance.
6. **[Gate Dynamics]** Observation deck and control room get the standard "window into a dangerous volume" kit: deepslate frame, copper sill, shutter rail, hazard stripe at the door; the six-shape gallery gets dark plinths, per-shape coloured under-lighting and named risers.
7. **[Ring Transit]** Band the 60-deep shaft in alternating light/dark 4-block courses, add hazard collars at every opening and a dim lit landing ring at the bottom.
8. **[Beam Physics]** Rebuild the pad array as a quartz-panel test chamber with grooved walls and under-lit copper pads, numbered on bollards.
9. **[End Annex]** Give the far End site the Atlantis treatment: oxidised copper, purpur, obsidian spines, a glass bridge, and a tall geometric stained-glass window behind the gate; orient it to a gateway beam.
10. **[Nether Range]** Build the Range into a repaired fortress/bastion silhouette, lit only by soul fire, with a chain-railed approach bridge; polished blackstone for its internal corridor module.

Honourable mentions: the briefing room as a third level over the control room [Ops]; one sculk "containment incident" alcove behind cyan glass [Beam or Mirror]; the two-lever interlock prop [Systems mezzanine]; MALP-garage service bays for the Motor Pool [Menagerie].

## 5. Schematic sources with permissive terms (not downloaded)

None of the Minecraft builds found carries a formal Creative Commons licence. The nearest things:

- **Trydar's Stargate Atlantis Project** — https://www.planetminecraft.com/project/trydars-stargate-atlantis-project/ — author's own statement on the page: free to use in any manner, on condition the credit sign (name and email) in the gate room is kept. Not a CC licence, and the download links are MediaFire from 2017 (one commenter reports them dead). If it is ever imported, keep the sign and treat the condition as binding. Only the gate-room interior is finished; the rest is a hollow shell, so it would be a proportions reference rather than a drop-in.
- **Fandom SGCommand wiki text** (Refs 7, 8) is CC BY-SA, and **Wikipedia** (Ref 9) is CC BY-SA 4.0 — fine to paraphrase with attribution for in-world lore books, but share-alike applies to any verbatim reuse.
- **Minecraft Wiki** text is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 (https://minecraft.wiki/w/Minecraft_Wiki:Copyrights) — non-commercial, share-alike.
- **Not permissive / unknown:** Dippy22's SGC recreation and Valicier's Stargate map (PMC default: creator copyright, no reuse statement); donut.build and createmod.com schematics (site terms, no CC licence visible; createmod's "Laboratory" entry asks for credit on republish, which is a request, not a licence); BuiltByBit and Gumroad sci-fi schematics are paid with their own licences; the rom1504 "minecraft-schematics-dataset" on GitHub/GitLab shows no licence in the README fetched and aggregates third-party schematics, so it cannot be assumed reusable.

Conclusion: nothing found is clean enough to import without a conversation with its author. Build from the ideas above.
